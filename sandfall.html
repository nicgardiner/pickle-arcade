4D Tetris — how to play

The board

The two wells side by side are not two boards. They are the two W slices of one 4×4×12×2 board: W 0 · kata on the left, W 1 · ana on the right. A well's rim lights cyan while the falling piece has cells in it — a piece can sit in both at once.

Pieces are the familiar seven tetrominoes, but they start flat and can be spun into any of the coordinate planes, so a rotation can push cells straight out of one well and into the other.

Clearing a layer

A layer is one height across both wells — 32 cells, eight whole pieces. It only clears when every one of those 32 is filled: packing one well perfectly clears nothing.

The ladder between the wells is the only readout of that. One bar per height, filling as the layer fills, and it turns gold the moment that height is full in both wells.

Scoring

1 layer — single 100 × level
2 layers — double 300 × level
3 layers — triple 500 × level
4 layers — HYPERTETRIS 800 × level
Soft drop / hard drop 1 per cell

A level every four layers, up to 20, and each one drops pieces faster. Four layers, not ten — a layer here is 32 cells.

The W-spin

Rotate through W as your last move, and land with the piece wedged — nowhere left to go in X, Z, W or up. That is a W-spin: 100 × level on the lock, and it doubles whatever that same lock clears. It is the one rule that pays you for using the fourth dimension instead of merely tolerating it.
